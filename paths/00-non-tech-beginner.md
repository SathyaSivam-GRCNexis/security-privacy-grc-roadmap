# Path: Non-Tech Beginner

> *You are figuring out whether security, privacy, or GRC is for you. No IT background. That is fine. Half the people I work with came from law, audit, customer success, journalism, or some random social-sciences degree. The field rewards clear writing and stubborn follow-through more than it rewards a CS degree.*

**Estimated time:** 80 to 100 hours over 10 to 14 weeks (8 to 10 hours per week).
**End state:** ready to apply for **GRC Analyst**, **Privacy Analyst**, **Compliance Associate**, **TPRM Analyst**, or **IT Audit Associate** roles.

---

## How hard is this, honestly?

Harder than the bootcamp blogs suggest. Easier than getting into software engineering from scratch.

The wall is the vocabulary, not the work. You will spend the first four weeks feeling like everyone is speaking in acronyms. SOC 2, ISO 27001, NIST, DPDP, GDPR, ROPA, DPIA, SoA, DDQ, TPRM. Then one week it clicks and the rest is reps. Most beginners quit somewhere in week three. If you survive that, you finish.

The honest hiring picture: entry GRC roles in India are competitive but not gated. Recruiters care about whether you can write a control description, fill an evidence binder, and not panic in a customer security review. None of that needs a degree. All of it needs practice.

---

## What wastes time vs. what compounds

What wastes time:

- Collecting certifications before you have anything to point at. A wall of badges with no portfolio reads as nervous, not credible.
- Watching YouTube playlists at 1.5x for 40 hours. Passive viewing does not stick. Half an hour of writing a fake policy beats four hours of watching.
- Memorising every clause of ISO 27001. Auditors do not memorise; they look it up. You should too.
- Tool obsession. Vanta, Drata, Secureframe, OneTrust. These are one-week onboarding once you know controls. Learn controls first.

What compounds:

- A real portfolio. A risk register you wrote. A policy you drafted. A SOC 2 explainer in plain English. Two capstone scenarios with deliverables. Pin these on GitHub or Notion and link from your CV. This single thing outperforms every certificate you can buy in year one.
- Writing in public. A weekly LinkedIn post on a real event (a CERT-In directive, a breach, a DPDP rules update) builds a search trail that recruiters find.
- Reading actual audit reports. Find a public SOC 2 Type II report. Read Section 4. Sit with the discomfort. Repeat.

## My take on certifications for beginners

Most of the alphabet soup is theatre at this stage. Useful order:

1. **ISC2 CC (Certified in Cybersecurity)**. Free. Take it in week four or five. It is a vocabulary check, not a credential. Treat it as such.
2. **CompTIA Security+**. Worth it if your CV has zero technical signal. Skip it if you have a portfolio that already speaks. Around USD 370. Not magical.
3. **CISSP**. Do not touch this on day one. You need five years of relevant experience to be certified anyway. Beginners chasing CISSP are the most reliable sign of someone who has not actually started the work.
4. **CIPP/E** or **ISO 27001 Lead Implementer**. Worth real money. Take after you have a job or a clear interview pipeline. Not before.

If you are torn between spending a weekend on Security+ flashcards or writing a policy and a risk register, write the policy and the risk register. Every time.

## What you can skip (and what you cannot)

You can skim or skip:

- **Module 02 (Cryptography)** beyond the "what each algorithm is for" table. You will not be implementing crypto.
- **Module 04 (Network Security)** beyond the basics in Module 0.2. Read for vocabulary. Do not memorise subnetting.
- **Module 13 (Cloud Security)** beyond the shared-responsibility model. Come back later.
- **Module 14 (AppSec)** beyond OWASP Top 10 awareness. Nobody hands a code review to a junior on week one.
- **Module 15 (Emerging Topics)** until you have a job. Skim for interview talking points. Do not try to master AI security in week two.

You cannot skip and must do well:

- **Module 0**. The entire foundation pack.
- **Module 1**. Security first principles (CIA, threat-modelling vocabulary).
- **Module 3**. Identity and access. Most of GRC sits on top of IAM.
- **Modules 5 to 10**. Privacy fundamentals through policies. **This is your core.** If you skip these you will fail your first interview.
- **Module 16**. Careers and interviews.
- **Module 17**. Pick at least three scenarios from 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 9 and do them properly. Properly means written deliverables, not bullet points.

If I had to name one module to double down on: **Module 7** (frameworks). Cross-mapping is the single skill that separates a junior who survives their first audit from one who gets quietly moved off the project.

## The 12-week schedule

| Week | Modules | What you will produce |
|------|---------|----------------------|
| 1 | Module 0 (full) | A one-page glossary in your own words |
| 2 | Module 1 (full) + Module 3 §3.1 to 3.3 | A short threat model of your favourite app |
| 3 | Module 5 (full) | A data inventory of your own digital life |
| 4 | Module 6, focus on GDPR + DPDP | A topic-by-law matrix (GDPR vs DPDP, your own table) |
| 5 | Module 6 finish + Module 7 §7.1 (SOC 2) | A one-page "what is SOC 2" explainer in plain English |
| 6 | Module 7: ISO 27001, NIST CSF, cross-mapping | Your own 8-row cross-map on a domain you choose |
| 7 | Module 8 (full) | A 10-row risk register on a fictional SaaS |
| 8 | Module 9 (full) | An evidence binder index for 5 controls |
| 9 | Module 10 (full) | One real policy you wrote (access control or data retention) |
| 10 | Module 16, re-read 16.3 portfolio + 16.5 interviews | CV rewritten; LinkedIn updated; 2 portfolio links pinned |
| 11 | Module 17, Scenarios 2 (DSR) + 4 (DDQ) | Both deliverables completed and put in your portfolio repo |
| 12 | Module 17, Scenario 6 (DPDP SDF) | Completed; plus mock interviews using [interview-prep/question-bank.md](../interview-prep/question-bank.md) |

If you can give six hours a week instead of ten, double the schedule to 24 weeks. Pace matters less than finishing.

## What success looks like at the end

You can:

- Define security, privacy, compliance, and GRC without confusing them.
- Read a SOC 2 Type II report and find the actual signal in Section 4.
- Walk through a SOC 2 audit lifecycle from kickoff to report.
- Map a control to SOC 2, ISO 27001:2022 Annex A, and NIST CSF 2.0.
- Respond to a GDPR Article 17 deletion request without panicking.
- Explain DPDP Act SDF obligations to a fintech CTO without bluffing.
- Build and defend a 10-row risk register in a room.
- Write a one-page access-control policy an auditor will accept.
- Discuss two completed capstone scenarios in an interview, not recite them.

That is a junior GRC analyst's interview-day toolkit. You are hireable.

## Roles you can credibly apply for

From [Module 16.1](../modules/16-careers-and-interviews.md):

- **GRC Analyst** (0 to 2 year band) at SaaS, GCCs, BFSI.
- **Privacy Analyst** at companies operationalising GDPR or DPDP.
- **IT Audit Associate** at Big 4 (Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG).
- **Compliance Associate** at fintech, edtech, healthtech.
- **TPRM Analyst** (Third-Party Risk Management). Underrated entry route. High volume of openings, lower competition than "Security Analyst" titles.
- **Security Awareness or Training Coordinator.**
- **Trust and Safety Analyst** at consumer internet firms.

India salary band for entry GRC roles, 2025 to 26, INR LPA: **6 to 12**. See [interview-prep/india-market-snapshot.md](../interview-prep/india-market-snapshot.md) for the fuller picture. Treat the upper end as a stretch, not a baseline.

---

## Two warnings before you start

**One.** Do not buy certifications before you have done the work. Free ISC2 CC is enough as a first credential. Save the money for CIPP/E or ISO 27001 Lead Implementer after you have a job offer or a real interview pipeline. The certification industry runs on beginner anxiety. Be aware of it.

**Two.** Do not chase tools. GRC platforms are easy to learn once you understand the underlying controls. A week of Vanta after you understand SOC 2 is enough. A month of Vanta without understanding SOC 2 teaches you nothing transferable.
