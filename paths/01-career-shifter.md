# Path: Career-Shifter

> *You have a career already. Finance, audit, law, project management, operations, HR, customer success, or IT support. You want to move into security, privacy, or GRC properly. You are not starting over. You are bridging. The audit instincts, drafting precision, stakeholder fluency, and process discipline you already have are most of the job. What you need is the vocabulary and a portfolio that lets a hiring manager skip the "is this person serious" question.*

**Estimated time:** 140 to 180 hours over 16 to 20 weeks (8 to 10 hours per week).
**End state:** ready to apply for **Senior GRC Analyst**, **Privacy Manager**, **IT Auditor**, **Compliance Manager**, **TPRM Lead**, or **Security Awareness Lead** roles, with a portfolio that shows depth.

---

## How hard is this, honestly?

Easier than the non-tech beginner path. Harder than people coming off it expect.

The work is not the wall. You already know how to read a regulation, sit in a control walkthrough, and write a memo. The wall is two-fold:

1. Recruiters use keyword filters. If your CV says "Statutory Audit Senior" and not "SOC 2", "ISO 27001", or "DPDP", you do not get the call regardless of how transferable your skills are. Fix the language and you get screened in.
2. You will take a step back in title before you go forward. A 6-year senior auditor often lands as a Senior GRC Analyst, not Manager, on the first jump. Plan for 12 to 18 months at the lower title, then leapfrog. People who refuse the title cut stay stuck.

The good news: once you have one GRC role under the belt and a real cert, your prior experience compounds fast. Mid-career switchers in India often hit Manager titles inside two years.

---

## What wastes time vs. what compounds

**Most career-shifters waste the first three months on the wrong certification.** Usually CISSP (cannot certify without five years in security domains) or CISA studied in isolation without any GRC artefacts to show. Both end up as a bullet on a CV that hiring managers ignore because there is nothing behind it.

The pattern that works: **one strong cert plus reps**. Pick the cert that matches your direction (ISO 27001 LI for SaaS or consulting; CISA if you are bridging from audit; CIPP/E if you are bridging from law). Then spend equal time producing artefacts. Each artefact is a rep. Reps stack. Certs alone do not.

What wastes time:

- Stacking two beginner certs (CC + Security+) before you do anything substantive. Either is fine. Both is not twice as good.
- Reading every regulation cover to cover. Read the parts that show up in audits. The rest you look up when needed.
- Building your portfolio inside your current employer's tooling without exporting redacted versions for yourself. You will leave. Take the artefacts with you.

What compounds:

- A portfolio with five or more artefacts a hiring manager can open in 30 seconds. Risk register, policy set, DPIA, SoA, vendor DDQ. Public on GitHub or Notion.
- A short LinkedIn write-up every 10 days on a real regulatory event tied to your prior industry. A statutory auditor writing on DPDP for banks reads as credible. The same person posting generic "5 tips for SOC 2" reads like everyone else.
- One serious certification finished, not three half-started.

## How your background actually maps

| Coming from | Maps strongly to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Audit (statutory or internal)** | IT Audit, GRC, ISO LA, SOX IT | Evidence collection, working-paper discipline |
| **Finance or Accounting** | SOX, SOC 1, BFSI compliance, FAIR-style risk | ICFR vocabulary, materiality thinking |
| **Law or Paralegal** | Privacy (DPO, DPM), Trust and Safety, Policy | Statutory interpretation, drafting precision |
| **Project or Programme Mgmt** | GRC programme lead, Audit lead, BCP | Stakeholder, timeline, and dependency thinking |
| **Customer Success or Support** | TPRM, Trust and Safety, GRC, Privacy ops | Communication and escalation discipline |
| **HR** | Insider risk, awareness, BCP, policy | People-process fluency |
| **IT Support or Helpdesk** | SOC L1, IAM Ops, GRC, vendor risk | Existing systems intuition |
| **Operations or BPO** | Process risk, SOC 2 controls, vendor mgmt | SOP and control-design instinct |

Lean on your background in interviews. The candidate who says "I led eight statutory audits and I want to apply that evidence discipline to ISO 27001" is more credible than the one who says "I just did the Google Cybersecurity Certificate." First-person stories beat generic study any day.

## Your portfolio target by week 18

Per [Module 16.3](../modules/16-careers-and-interviews.md), your GitHub or Notion should hold:

- **A risk register** with 15+ rows, scored, owners assigned, treatments documented.
- **A DPIA** for the AI feature scenario (Scenario 7) or the SDF scenario (Scenario 6).
- **A Statement of Applicability** covering at least 20 ISO 27001:2022 Annex A controls.
- **An IR runbook** for one playbook of your choice.
- **A vendor assessment** with tiering, DDQ response, and a two-page memo with findings.
- **A policy set** (3 policies, 1 standard, 1 procedure) mapped to ISO 27001 and SOC 2.
- **A cross-mapping spreadsheet** (12 rows, 5 frameworks).
- **5 to 10 LinkedIn write-ups** on real regulatory events: a CERT-In directive, a public breach, the DPDP Rules, an EU AI Act milestone.

This is more demonstrable than what most two-year GRC analysts have. Use it shamelessly.

## Certification path, in order

Pick one cert at a time. Finish it before starting the next. Do not study three in parallel; you will pass none.

1. **ISC2 CC**. Free. Get it in week four. Vocabulary check.
2. **CompTIA Security+**. Paid, around USD 370. Optional. Take after Module 7 only if your CV has no security keyword anywhere.
3. **ISO 27001:2022 Lead Implementer** (PECB or BSI). The most useful single GRC cert for India and EMEA SaaS work. Take after Module 10. This is the one I would not skip.
4. **CISA** (ISACA). Gold standard for IT audit. Take six to twelve months in. Effectively required at Big 4.
5. **CIPP/E** (IAPP). If your trajectory is privacy-heavy. Take after Module 6 mastery.
6. **CRISC, CISM, CISSP**. Later. These are ceiling-raisers, not starter certs. Touching them too early signals you do not understand the market.

Per [Module 16.2](../modules/16-careers-and-interviews.md): in India, **ISO 27001 LI + CISA** remains the most employable GRC combo at Big 4, GCCs, and SaaS.

## Salary expectation, honestly

India 2025 to 26, INR LPA. See [interview-prep/india-market-snapshot.md](../interview-prep/india-market-snapshot.md):

- Mid-career switcher landing first GRC role: **8 to 15**, depending on how much prior experience the recruiter agrees to count.
- After 18 months with ISO LI + CISA: **15 to 25**.
- Privacy Manager or Senior GRC at 4 to 6 years total experience: **18 to 35**.

Remote-first global roles often offer 1.8x to 3x India-local bands. Many switchers lift compensation faster than they expect once the certs and portfolio are in place. Expect the first jump to feel like a step back on title and a step forward on pay; that trade is usually worth it.

## One mindset shift before you begin

You are not starting over. You are redeploying evidence skills, audit instincts, and stakeholder fluency into a domain with new vocabulary and tighter clocks. Treat this curriculum as the vocabulary acquisition, not a personality change. Your strongest interview answers will start: *"In my previous role I handled X, and I would apply the same approach here by..."*
