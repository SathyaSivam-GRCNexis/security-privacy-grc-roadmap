## Step 1 — Which of these sounds like you?

Tick the one that fits best. If two fit, pick the one that fits *most*.

### 🟢 You are a non-tech beginner

You don't write code. You may have never worked in IT. You've heard "cybersecurity is hot" and want to find out whether it's for you, what the actual jobs look like, and whether you can do them without a CS degree. You can absolutely do this. Many of the strongest people in GRC and privacy came in from law, social sciences, journalism, customer support, or completely unrelated fields.

→ Read **[paths/00-non-tech-beginner.md](paths/00-non-tech-beginner.md)**

### 🟡 You are a career-shifter

You have a few years of professional experience somewhere — finance, audit, project management, operations, HR, legal, IT support, customer success — and you want to move into security, privacy, or GRC as a real career. You bring transferable skills (writing, stakeholder management, process discipline, audit literacy). What you need is the domain language and the artefacts that prove you can do the work.

→ Read **[paths/01-career-shifter.md](paths/01-career-shifter.md)**

### 🔴 You are a tech professional

You are a software engineer, SRE, cloud engineer, DBA, QA, or platform engineer. You know systems. You want to specialise into security engineering, cloud security, AppSec, DevSecOps, or move sideways into GRC engineering. You can skim the foundations fast and spend most of your time on cloud, AppSec, frameworks, and emerging topics.

→ Read **[paths/02-tech-professional.md](paths/02-tech-professional.md)**

## Step 2 — Three honest expectations

**One.** This will take you between 80 and 180 hours of focused work, depending on your starting point. Spread over 10 to 20 weeks at 8–10 hours per week, that's a fair commitment. There is no faster real route.

**Two.** Watching is not learning. Every module ends with a mini-exercise and most have a worked example. Do the work. The people who do the exercises get jobs. The people who only watch get certificates and confusion.

**Three.** You will produce **portfolio artefacts** — a risk register, a DPIA, a SoA, an IR runbook, a vendor assessment. These are the things hiring managers actually ask to see. Three completed capstone scenarios from [Module 17](modules/17-practice-scenarios.md) out-compete five certificates on a resume.

## Step 3 — How the modules are designed

Every module follows the same shape, so you stop guessing what comes next:

1. **Why this matters** — the business reason, before the theory.
2. **Core definitions** — the terms, in plain English.
3. **Real-world analogy** — to make it stick.
4. **Concept-by-concept teaching** — small steps, in order.
5. **Worked example** — usually around a fictional EdTech SaaS.
6. **Comparison table** — side-by-side, so you remember the differences.
7. **Common beginner mistakes** — the traps your peers will fall into.
8. **Interview traps** — the questions that catch people out.
9. **Mini-exercise** — 10 to 45 minutes of doing.
10. **Go deeper** — curated free resources, with a legend (🏛 official · 📘 free book/course · 📰 article · 🎥 video · 🧪 lab · 💰 paid but worth it).
11. **Glossary recap** — every term you should now own.
12. **Next:** — pointer to what's next.

## Step 4 — How to use this repo if you only have 20 minutes a day

Open [modules/](modules/), start at Module 0, read one section per sitting, do the mini-exercise that weekend. That pace finishes the curriculum in about 4 months. It's enough.
