## Step 1, Which of these sounds like you?

Tick the one that fits best. If two fit, pick the one that fits *most*. The biggest time-waste I see is people picking the wrong path and then wondering why Module 9 feels like reading Greek.

### 🟢 You are a non-tech beginner

You do not write code. You may have never worked in IT. You have heard "cybersecurity is hot" and want to find out whether it is for you, what the actual jobs look like, and whether you can do them without a CS degree. You can. Some of the strongest people I have worked with in GRC and privacy came in from law, social sciences, journalism, customer support, or completely unrelated fields. The skill that travels is reading a regulation, asking the awkward question, and writing it down clearly.

→ Read **[paths/00-non-tech-beginner.md](paths/00-non-tech-beginner.md)**

### 🟡 You are a career-shifter

You have a few years of professional experience somewhere. Finance, audit, project management, operations, HR, legal, IT support, customer success. You want to move into security, privacy, or GRC as a real career. You bring transferable skills (writing, stakeholder management, process discipline, audit literacy). What you need is the domain language and the artefacts that prove you can do the work. Usually this is the fastest path of the three, because the transferable skills are the ones that take years to build.

→ Read **[paths/01-career-shifter.md](paths/01-career-shifter.md)**

### 🔴 You are a tech professional

You are a software engineer, SRE, cloud engineer, DBA, QA, or platform engineer. You know systems. You want to specialise into security engineering, cloud security, AppSec, DevSecOps, or move sideways into GRC engineering. Skim the foundations. Spend most of your time on cloud, AppSec, frameworks, and the emerging-topics modules. Honest warning: if you are moving into GRC, the culture shock is real. Less code, more meetings, more writing.

→ Read **[paths/02-tech-professional.md](paths/02-tech-professional.md)**

## Step 2, Three honest expectations

**One.** This will take you between 80 and 180 hours of focused work, depending on your starting point. Spread over 10 to 20 weeks at 8 to 10 hours per week, that is a fair commitment. There is no faster real route. The four-week bootcamps lie.

**Two.** Watching is not learning. Every module ends with a mini-exercise and most have a worked example. Do the work. In my experience, the people who do the exercises get jobs. The people who only watch get certificates and confusion.

**Three.** You will produce **portfolio artefacts**. A risk register, a DPIA, a SoA, an IR runbook, a vendor assessment. These are the things hiring managers actually ask to see. Three completed capstone scenarios from [Module 17](modules/17-practice-scenarios.md) will outcompete five certificates on a CV, almost every time.

## Step 3, How the modules are designed

Every module follows the same shape, so you stop guessing what comes next:

1. **Why this matters**, the business reason, before the theory.
2. **Core definitions**, the terms, in plain English.
3. **Real-world analogy**, to make it stick.
4. **Concept-by-concept teaching**, small steps, in order.
5. **Worked example**, usually around a fictional EdTech SaaS.
6. **Comparison table**, side-by-side, so you remember the differences.
7. **Common beginner mistakes**, the traps your peers will fall into.
8. **Interview traps**, the questions that catch people out.
9. **Mini-exercise**, 10 to 45 minutes of doing.
10. **Go deeper**, curated free resources, with a legend (🏛 official · 📘 free book/course · 📰 article · 🎥 video · 🧪 lab · 💰 paid but worth it).
11. **Glossary recap**, every term you should now own.
12. **Next:**, pointer to what comes after.

## Step 4, How to use this repo if you only have 20 minutes a day

Open [modules/](modules/), start at Module 0, read one section per sitting, do the mini-exercise that weekend. That pace finishes the curriculum in about four months. That is enough. Most of the people I have mentored stalled because they tried to do four hours on a Sunday and burned out by week three. Twenty minutes every day beats four hours once a week.

## Step 5, Where people typically stall

A few patterns to watch out for, because I have seen them play out repeatedly:

- **Module 5 (privacy law) feels heavy.** It is. Slow down, do not skim, and read the regulator guidance (ICO, EDPB) rather than the raw legal text.
- **Module 7 (frameworks) feels like alphabet soup.** SOC 2, ISO 27001, NIST CSF, PCI, HIPAA. The cross-map reference is your friend. Most of the time, the controls overlap.
- **Cert-chasing.** People stop doing the modules and start collecting badges. Certifications open doors. They do not get you the job. The job is still about chasing evidence.
